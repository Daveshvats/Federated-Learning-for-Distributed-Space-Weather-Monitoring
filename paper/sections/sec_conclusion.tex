\section{Conclusion}\label{sec:conclusion}

We presented what the documented literature search of
Section~\ref{sec:litsearch} indicates is the first application of
federated learning to solar flare prediction, motivated by a plausible
deployment constraint in operational space weather: several public
data custodians restrict redistribution of raw magnetogram time
series, so the strongest pooled models may not be trainable at all
unless the custodians federate. The proposed framework simulates that
constraint on the public SWAN-SF benchmark---six regional clients
(neutral labels, no real institution) holding disjoint
Dirichlet-skewed shards, MLP clients trained under FedAvg and FedProx
with a clamped server-aware focal loss, and a frozen, leakage-audited
evaluation protocol whose every selection decision is made on
validation data. The study is an upstream flare-probability component
for grid-resilience pipelines; the downstream flare$\to$CME$\to$storm
$\to$GIC chain is discussed as future integration, not evaluated.

To keep the summary honest, we classify the paper's claims into three
levels, tiered against the leakage-free fold of
Section~\ref{sec:res-leakfree}. \textbf{Demonstrated (leakage-free,
single-pass, frozen protocol)}: the cleaned SWAN-SF export's
same-partition train/test pairing shares instances (100\% of flaring
test windows; 85.7--90\% TimeGAN-synthetic training positives), and
the benchmark's intended temporally-preceding protocol removes the
overlap; on that protocol all arms generalise (ROC-AUC 0.906--0.978);
the in-partition federated-versus-centralised gap does not survive
(FedProx 0.976/0.308 vs architecture-matched centralised MLP
0.973/0.382); FedProx retains checkpoint-independent ROC stabilisation
over plain FedAvg (0.976 vs 0.906); the threshold-transfer and
calibration failures persist (only XGBoost's posteriors are usable);
and event-level detection is essentially solved for every arm (64--65
of 65 events, 23.4\,h median lead) while the false-alarm burden
separates the arms by roughly $7\times$.
\textbf{Demonstrated in-partition only (protocol-stability evidence,
disclosed overlap)}: FedProx with $\mu{=}0.01$ is the most \emph{consistent}
stabiliser among the components studied---above FedAvg on PR-AUC on
five of five seeds (one-sided Wilcoxon $p=0.031$) with $2.7\times$
narrower ROC-AUC variance, where the leakage-free fold shows the PR
ordering between the two FL arms reverses; the FedProx global model
attains 97.7\% of the centralised XGBoost reference's in-partition
ROC-AUC (0.954 vs 0.977); plain FedAvg diverges under the
preregistered configuration (and completes without collapse at
$\alpha{=}5.0$, so the divergence is a heterogeneity-severity effect);
per-client SMOTE is inactive on this benchmark variant (zero
synthetic samples); and both the centralised and federated models
concentrate attribution on current helicity, flux-emergence proxies,
and Lorentz-force terms.
\textbf{Supported with caveats}: one FedAvg cell with
distribution-aware
aggregation exceeds the FedProx headline ROC-AUC, recall-at-FPR
figures are window-level statistics over correlated windows,
five-seed confidence intervals quantify seed variability only, and
the leakage-free fold is a single fold and seed (fold- and
seed-replication is queued owner-side compute). Two
formerly listed caveats were resolved by executed re-runs: the
optimisation-budget confound (the budget-matched 500-epoch re-run of
Section~\ref{sec:budget} shows the in-partition FedProx gap survives
parity, though the gap itself is an in-partition artifact) and
the within-distribution client holdouts (the untouched-holdout
evaluation of Section~\ref{sec:res-clients} shows the federation
benefit is larger under clean estimates).
\textbf{Not demonstrated}: cryptographic privacy (secure aggregation
and differential privacy are implemented but their accuracy cost is
unmeasured; poisoning defence is absent), leakage-free
multi-seed/ablation replication and the remaining temporally-preceding
folds (one fold executed here), deployment-grade
calibration (under the prior shift the neural posteriors
are worse than climatology on Brier and ECE, and the executed
five-arm comparison shows no validation-fit calibrator rescues them),
neural-arm false-alarm reduction at event level,
the downstream GIC/SCADA pipeline, federated LSTM and
SCAFFOLD evaluation, and results on the raw, unbalanced benchmark.
An independent re-execution from the public, byte-verified dataset
artefacts reproduces every in-partition headline metric exactly
(Section~\ref{sec:repro}); six items of the re-run
programme---the frozen-FPR report, the calibration arms, the
untouched holdouts, the budget-matched and $\alpha{=}5.0$ re-runs, the
raw-metadata provenance audit, and the event-level/leakage-free
evaluation on the temporally-preceding fold---have since been executed
with results in Section~\ref{sec:results}; the remaining items are
listed in
Section~\ref{sec:limitations}, each with the implemented-and-tested
protocol that would upgrade the claim.

For the smart-grid community the message is correspondingly
conditional: \emph{if} custodians of magnetogram data face
data-locality constraints, then at benchmark scale a proximal-regularised
federation keeps the federation converging where plain averaging
collapses, at negligible bandwidth, without any raw data
movement---while matching the same-architecture pooled model's
ranking is, on the leakage-free protocol, a property the pooled
reference shares rather than a federation advantage. For the
machine-learning community the study is a caution and a demonstration:
instance-level provenance audits can invert the conclusions drawn
from a widely used preprocessed benchmark, and the cross-silo regime
of the physical sciences has failure modes---prior mismatch, layered
imbalance handling, optimiser--variance-reduction interactions---that
the medical and mobile FL literature does not fully anticipate, and
that are worth studying in their own right.

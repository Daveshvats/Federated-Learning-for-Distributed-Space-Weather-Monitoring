\section{Conclusion}\label{sec:conclusion}

We presented what the documented literature search of
Section~\ref{sec:litsearch} indicates is the first application of
federated learning to solar flare prediction, motivated by a plausible
deployment constraint in operational space weather: several public
data custodians restrict redistribution of raw magnetogram time
series, so the strongest pooled models may not be trainable at all
unless the custodians federate. The proposed framework simulates that
constraint on the public SWAN-SF benchmark---six regional clients
(neutral labels, no real institution) holding disjoint
Dirichlet-skewed shards, MLP clients trained under FedAvg and FedProx
with a clamped server-aware focal loss, and a frozen, leakage-audited
evaluation protocol whose every selection decision is made on
validation data. The study is an upstream flare-probability component
for grid-resilience pipelines; the downstream flare$\to$CME$\to$storm
$\to$GIC chain is discussed as future integration, not evaluated.

To keep the summary honest, we classify the paper's claims into three
levels. \textbf{Demonstrated} (multi-seed, single-pass, frozen
protocol): FedProx with $\mu{=}0.01$ is the most \emph{consistent}
stabiliser among the components studied---above FedAvg on PR-AUC on
five of five seeds (one-sided Wilcoxon $p=0.031$) with $2.7\times$
narrower ROC-AUC variance; the FedProx global model attains 97.7\% of
the centralised XGBoost reference's ROC-AUC (0.954 vs 0.977) and did
not underperform the same-architecture pooled MLP (0.888) on this
benchmark and configuration; plain FedAvg diverges under the
preregistered configuration; per-client SMOTE is inactive on this
benchmark variant (zero synthetic samples); and both the centralised
and federated models concentrate attribution on current helicity,
flux-emergence proxies, and Lorentz-force terms.
\textbf{Supported with caveats}: the FedProx-versus-centralised-MLP
comparison is confounded by optimisation budget (the federated arm
consumed more passes), one FedAvg cell with distribution-aware
aggregation exceeds the FedProx headline ROC-AUC, the client-level
comparison evaluates within-distribution holdouts, recall-at-FPR
figures are window-level statistics over correlated windows, and
five-seed confidence intervals quantify seed variability only.
\textbf{Not demonstrated}: cryptographic privacy (secure aggregation
and differential privacy are implemented but their accuracy cost is
unmeasured; poisoning defence is absent), region-disjoint
generalisation (the cleaned export lacks region identifiers;
the split generator awaits raw metadata), deployment-grade
calibration (under the $26\times$ prior shift the neural posteriors
are worse than climatology on Brier and ECE), event-level operational
performance, the downstream GIC/SCADA pipeline, federated LSTM and
SCAFFOLD evaluation, and results on the raw, unbalanced benchmark.
The queued re-run programme of Section~\ref{sec:limitations} lists,
for each of these, the implemented-and-tested protocol that would
upgrade the claim.

For the smart-grid community the message is correspondingly
conditional: \emph{if} custodians of magnetogram data face
data-locality constraints, then at benchmark scale a proximal-regularised
federation retains the ranking skill of a same-architecture pooled
model in this study, at negligible bandwidth, without any raw data
movement---and the released framework, with its frozen protocol,
machine-readable artefacts, and documented engineering history
(v2.1--v3.1), is intended to make the follow-up work a matter of
execution rather than re-engineering. For the machine-learning
community it is a demonstration that the cross-silo regime of the
physical sciences has failure modes---prior mismatch, layered
imbalance handling, optimiser--variance-reduction interactions---that
the medical and mobile FL literature does not fully anticipate, and
that are worth studying in their own right.

\section{Limitations and Future Work}\label{sec:limitations}

\paragraph{Privacy scope.}
This work enforces the data-sovereignty constraint (raw data never
leaves the agency), which is the binding constraint of the
space-weather setting, but the stronger cryptographic notion of
privacy is not yet addressed. The communication transcript of
Eq.~\eqref{eq:privacy}---gradients or weight deltas---is known to leak
training information under gradient-inversion attacks
\cite{zhu2019}, particularly for over-parameterised models on
low-entropy data. Before any cross-agency deployment handling
genuinely sensitive observations, the natural next steps are secure
aggregation (the server sees only the sum of updates) and differential
privacy in the local update \cite{kairouz2021}; both compose cleanly
with the FedProx loop used here and are standard engineering, but they
carry accuracy budgets that would need to be re-measured on this
benchmark.

\paragraph{Threshold selection and calibration.}
The reported thresholds are selected directly on the held-out test
set, mirroring the released evaluation code; the stricter protocol
would select $\tau^\ast$ on a validation split disjoint from the
reporting test set. Given that threshold selection chooses one scalar
from an 81-point grid, the optimism this injects is limited, but a
re-run with proper threshold validation is the single easiest
strengthening of the reported numbers. More fundamentally, the
always-positive behaviour during training
(Fig.~\ref{fig:convergence}) is a calibration defect, and principled
prior-correction methods (logit adjustment to the deployment prior, or
post-hoc Platt scaling at the server) should replace or complement
the threshold sweep in future versions.

\paragraph{Partition realism and disjointness.}
Two aspects of the partition simulation invite refinement. First, the
Dirichlet sampler in the released implementation draws class
sub-samples independently per client, so shards are not guaranteed
mutually disjoint; the realised overlap is a small fraction under the
released seed but should be eliminated by index bookkeeping for a
strict partition semantics. Second, geographic realism would map
active regions to the agencies whose instruments actually observed
them (HARP-tracking metadata supports this), replacing statistical
label skew with instrument- and coverage-driven skew.

\paragraph{Algorithm coverage.}
The committed artefacts cover the MLP path; the LSTM path---which
consumes the raw $60\times24$ windows and matches the recent temporal
architectures that lead centralised SWAN-SF benchmarks
\cite{hassani2025}---is fully implemented but not yet evaluated
federated, and SCAFFOLD is disabled pending the SGD-stability work
described in Appendix~\ref{sec:appendix}. Both are near-term
experiments: the LSTM is the most likely route to closing the F1 gap
to the tree-ensemble upper bound, and SCAFFOLD is the natural third
point on the drift-correction axis between FedAvg and FedProx.
Personalised FL (per-client heads over a shared backbone, in the
spirit of Ditto) is a third direction: agencies may prefer a global
model for situational awareness plus a locally adapted model for their
own asset fleet.

\paragraph{Single-seed, single-benchmark scope.}
The reported numbers are one run of one configuration on one
benchmark. Seed variance in FL is material, and the appropriate
follow-up is a five-seed repetition with confidence intervals on all
metrics of Table~\ref{tab:main}, plus a sweep over $\mu \in \{0.001,
0.01, 0.1\}$ and $\alpha \in \{0.5, 1.0, 5.0\}$ to map the
stability boundary of Appendix~\ref{sec:appendix} quantitatively. The
benchmark itself, and its cleaned variant, encode particular
preprocessing choices (resampling, synthetic augmentation,
normalisation) that shape the class-prior mismatch at the centre of
the calibration story; a complementary evaluation on the raw
unbalanced SWAN-SF partitions would separate benchmark-construction
effects from federation effects.

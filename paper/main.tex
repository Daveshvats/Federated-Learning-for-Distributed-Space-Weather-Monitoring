\textbf{Abstract ---}
The vulnerability of modern power grids to geomagnetically induced
currents (GICs), triggered by M- and X-class solar flares, presents a
critical and recurring threat to bulk electric infrastructure; the 1989
Qu\'ebec system collapse remains the canonical demonstration of how a
single geomagnetic storm can disable an entire provincial grid.
Effective early warning therefore requires predictive models trained
on magnetogram-derived feature histories, and the framework developed
here targets a deployment scenario in which participating institutions
impose data-locality or data-sharing constraints that prevent pooling
raw observations. The present study \emph{simulates} that constraint on
the public SWAN-SF benchmark; it does not demonstrate that any specific
agency's data cannot be pooled. We present, to the best of our
knowledge and per a documented search protocol
(Section~\ref{sec:related}), the first federated treatment of solar
flare prediction: six \emph{simulated} regional clients---hypothetical
custodians labelled by region, not participating institutions---hold
disjoint, audited Dirichlet-skewed shards of the benchmark. Each client
trains a local neural model on 144 statistical features derived from 24
photospheric magnetic-field parameters and transmits only weight
updates. All results are produced under a frozen evaluation protocol:
an immutable train/validation/test contract, calibration and threshold
selection on validation only, a runtime leakage audit, and single-pass
test evaluation. On the held-out test set (331{,}185 windows at the
natural 1.88\% flare prevalence), FedProx attains a ROC-AUC of 0.954
(97.7\% of the centralised XGBoost reference's 0.977) and a PR-AUC of
0.307, and did not underperform the pooled centralised MLP of
identical architecture (0.888 / 0.153) on this benchmark and
configuration---an empirical observation we discuss as a
regularisation-like effect, noting that the two arms received
different optimisation budgets, rather than as evidence that
federation is free of cost. Plain FedAvg diverges under the
preregistered headline configuration (validation AUC $0.99\to0.05$
after round 20). FedProx provided the most consistent stability under
that configuration, exceeding FedAvg's PR-AUC on five of five seeds
(one-sided Wilcoxon $p=0.031$) with $2.7\times$ narrower metric
variance; however, the 24-cell ablation shows that distribution-aware
aggregation also substantially mitigates FedAvg degradation, so
stability is not attributable to the proximal term alone. The
balancing arm (per-client SMOTE) is \emph{inactive} on the pre-balanced
benchmark (zero synthetic samples; reported as a null). Cross-model
SHAP analysis shows both the centralised and federated models
concentrate decisions on current helicity, the R-value flux-emergence
proxy, and Lorentz-force terms. We document a threshold-transfer
failure under the $26\times$ validation-to-test prevalence shift and
report prevalence-robust recall-at-FPR discrimination statistics
(FedProx: 0.51 recall at 2\% FPR); deployment thresholds for fixed-FPR
operation are selected on validation negatives and frozen before the
single test pass. An independent re-execution from the public dataset
artefacts (byte-verified against the SHA-256 data manifest)
reproduces every headline metric exactly; in the same session a
five-arm calibration comparison shows that \emph{no}
validation-fit calibrator survives the prior shift, and an
untouched-holdout study confirms that the proximal-stabilised global
model matches local-only performance for every simulated client while
clearly aiding the two smallest. The verified property is data locality, not
cryptographic privacy, and the model is an upstream flare-probability
component: the flare$\to$CME$\to$storm$\to$GIC chain, SCADA
integration, and event-level operational evaluation remain future
work.

\textbf{Abstract ---}
The vulnerability of modern power grids to geomagnetically induced
currents (GICs), triggered by M- and X-class solar flares, presents a
critical and recurring threat to bulk electric infrastructure; the 1989
Qu\'ebec system collapse remains the canonical demonstration of how a
single geomagnetic storm can disable an entire provincial grid.
Effective early warning therefore requires predictive models trained
on magnetogram-derived feature histories, and the framework developed
here targets a deployment scenario in which participating institutions
impose data-locality or data-sharing constraints that prevent pooling
raw observations. The present study \emph{simulates} that constraint on
the public SWAN-SF benchmark; it does not demonstrate that any specific
agency's data cannot be pooled. We present, to the best of our
knowledge and per a documented search protocol
(Section~\ref{sec:related}), the first federated treatment of solar
flare prediction: six \emph{simulated} regional clients---hypothetical
custodians labelled by region, not participating institutions---hold
disjoint, audited Dirichlet-skewed shards of the benchmark. Each client
trains a local neural model on 144 statistical features derived from 24
photospheric magnetic-field parameters and transmits only weight
updates. All results are produced under a frozen evaluation protocol:
an immutable train/validation/test contract, calibration and threshold
selection on validation only, a runtime leakage audit, and single-pass
test evaluation. A data-provenance audit against the raw benchmark
(Harvard Dataverse; every cleaned window aligned to its raw instance
by normalization-invariant matching, 98.7--100\% verification with
100\% label agreement) then found that the cleaned export's
same-partition train/test pairing shares instances: the test export of
each partition contains \emph{all} raw instances while the training
export is a rebalanced subset of the same instances, so 100\% of the
flaring test windows (6{,}234) and 99.998\% of verified training
windows were also test windows, and 85.7--90\% of training positives
are TimeGAN-synthetic. The study therefore re-evaluates every headline
claim on a leakage-free fold that follows the benchmark's intended
temporally-preceding protocol (training partitions 1--4, single-pass
test on partition 5, natural 1.31\% prevalence, identical frozen
protocol). On that fold, discrimination generalises for all arms
(ROC-AUC 0.906--0.978), the in-partition federated-versus-centralised
gap disappears (FedProx 0.976 / PR 0.308 vs centralised MLP of
identical architecture 0.973 / 0.382), and FedProx retains a ROC
stabilisation over plain FedAvg (0.976 vs 0.906) whose val-curve
dynamics are checkpoint-independent, although FedAvg's PR-AUC is
higher (0.370 vs 0.308). Finally, retraining the identical frozen
protocol on the RAW, unbalanced benchmark (natural 2.05\% training
prevalence, no synthetic positives, the release's FPCKNN imputation
and LSBZM normalisation reproduced in-pipeline with train-only
parameters, verified against the released export) separates the arms
decisively: the centralised baselines are substrate-robust (LR
unchanged at 0.978; centralised MLP PR 0.445) while every federated
\emph{MLP} arm degrades sharply (FedProx 0.930/0.149; FedAvg detects
zero of 65 events; SCAFFOLD fails at 0.768/0.057). The collapse is
encoder-conditional: under the same protocol the sequential SolarLSTM
(owner GPU) does not collapse---FedAvg-LSTM 0.958/0.305 against its
MLP counterpart's 0.875/0.056, FedProx-LSTM 0.970/0.404, above its
pooled comparator though below logistic regression---so the
balanced-substrate standing is a property of the flattened-feature
MLP, not of federation per se; the case for federating this problem
rests on data locality, not performance. Plain FedAvg diverges under the
preregistered headline configuration (validation AUC $0.99\to0.05$
after round 20; the in-partition multiseed, sweep, ablation and
client-level studies are retained as protocol-stability evidence and
explicitly not as generalisation estimates). Cross-model SHAP analysis
shows both the centralised and federated models concentrate decisions
on current helicity, the R-value flux-emergence proxy, and
Lorentz-force terms. We document a threshold-transfer failure that
persists leakage-free under the prevalence shift and report
prevalence-robust recall-at-FPR discrimination statistics; an
event-level evaluation on 65 physical M/X-class events (raw-benchmark
event keys, 1-hour alert cooldown) shows all arms detect 98.5--100\%
of events with a 23.4-hour median lead time to flare peak, while
XGBoost's false-alarm burden (1.7 windows/day) is roughly $7\times$
lower than every neural arm's---the deployability boundary on this
benchmark is calibration, not detection. An independent re-execution
from the public dataset artefacts (byte-verified against the SHA-256
data manifest) reproduces every in-partition headline metric exactly;
in the same session a five-arm calibration comparison shows that
\emph{no} validation-fit calibrator survives the prior shift, and an
untouched-holdout study confirms that the proximal-stabilised global
model matches local-only performance for every simulated client while
clearly aiding the two smallest. The verified property is data locality, not
cryptographic privacy, and the model is an upstream flare-probability
component: the flare$\to$CME$\to$storm$\to$GIC chain and SCADA
integration remain future work.

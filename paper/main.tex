\textbf{Abstract ---}
The vulnerability of modern power grids to geomagnetically induced
currents (GICs), triggered by M- and X-class solar flares, presents a
critical and recurring threat to bulk electric infrastructure; the 1989
Qu\'ebec system collapse remains the canonical demonstration of how a
single geomagnetic storm can disable an entire provincial grid.
Effective early-warning systems require predictive models trained on
high-fidelity solar observations spanning multiple geographic regions,
yet nationally sensitive space-weather data collected by agencies such
as NASA, ESA, JAXA, ISRO, KASI, and the Australian Bureau of
Meteorology cannot be centralised because of data-sovereignty
constraints and inter-agency data-sharing agreements. This paper
presents, to the best of our knowledge, the first application of
federated learning (FL) to solar flare prediction for smart-grid
protection, explicitly addressing this privacy barrier. The proposed
framework partitions the SWAN-SF benchmark dataset into six
geographically distributed regional-observatory clients using Dirichlet
sampling, inducing realistic non-IID label skew. Each client trains a
local neural model on 144 statistical features derived from 24
photospheric magnetic-field parameters, and transmits only weight
updates---never raw satellite measurements---to an aggregation server
that combines FedAvg and FedProx (proximal coefficient
$\mu = 0.01$) with distribution-aware weighting. On a held-out test set
that preserves the natural $\sim$1.9\% flare prevalence, FedProx attains
an ROC-AUC of 0.930 (95.0\% of the centralised XGBoost upper bound of
0.978), a recall of 0.416, and an F1-score of 0.326 at an
$F_\beta$-optimal decision threshold ($\beta=2$), whereas FedAvg
degenerates under the same partition. SHAP analysis of the centralised
surrogate identifies extrema of the flux-emergence proxy
(R-value), current helicity, and Lorentz-force variability as the
dominant predictors, consistent with established solar physics. The
framework converges within 50 communication rounds, making it
operationally compatible with existing SCADA-based grid-management
infrastructure. These results demonstrate that distributed space-weather
agencies can jointly train a usable flare-prediction model without
compromising data sovereignty, establishing federated machine learning
as a practical mechanism for global smart-grid resilience against
geomagnetic threats.

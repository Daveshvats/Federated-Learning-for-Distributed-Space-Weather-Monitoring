**Conceded in full; the over-correction is undone.** The panel is right that the v4.4 fix traded a silent shadow for a loud refusal at the repo's front door: because `logs/test_battery_v4.4.log` was committed, every fresh clone's first run of the documented verification command exited 1 at the guard — fail-safe, self-explaining, and still a front door that refuses its own entry point. The fix takes the panel's first option: the log is un-shipped (`git rm`), and `logs/test_battery_v*.log` is gitignored so no future version's log can ship at the default write path — the run record for current versions lives in the RUNLOG row (this one carries the v4.5 count and per-file split), while the frozen historical logs (canonical v4.1 at `logs/test_battery.log`, plus v4.2 and v4.3) stay committed and are never write targets. The docstring of `tests/run_battery.py` now states the first-run contract plainly, and the refreshed root README — the compounding factor the panel cited — documents the battery, the guard, and the log convention at the front door.

## R7-2 — the budget artefact's federated batch size

**Conceded in full; regenerated with the generator, not hand-patched.** `outputs/results.json` recorded the federated batch size as 256 because `main.py` passed `cfg.BATCH_SIZE` to the budget generator while the federated loader — since v3.0.1 — has trained at a hardcoded 512 that the config constant never reached: two sources of truth, and the artefact inherited the stale one while the paper's tables (512; 256 & 512) inherited the true one. The defect chain is closed at both ends. The single source of truth is now `LOCAL_BATCH_SIZE = 512` in `federated_learning.py` (the `_FastLoader` default and `main.py`'s report both cite it; the dead `cfg.BATCH_SIZE` is deleted from `config.py` with a comment recording why). The artefact field was regenerated by the block's own generator with the same arguments `main.py` passes, `fl_batch` corrected — and the provenance is machine-checked, not asserted: the generator reproduces the committed block **exactly** when fed the stale 256, the patched block equals the generator's output when fed 512, and the JSON-level diff is confined to `training_budget.federated.batch_size` (sha256 `aa03f0c266f9a1df` → `097108bf8093ead2`, both recorded in the RUNLOG). The paper needed no change; its tables were already correct on both sides.

## R7-3 — the SCAFFOLD optimiser and learning rate were undisclosed

**Conceded in full; the parenthetical is in, the dead constant is out.** The methods section's SCAFFOLD paragraph now discloses the shipped optimiser — SGD with momentum 0.9 at a learning rate of 2.5×10⁻⁴, half the global rate — and states the consequence in the panel's own terms: momentum biases the average-gradient estimate that the control-variate update divides by, a fourth departure of the same, milder class as the disclosed AdamW incompatibility. One parenthetical, one deletion, exactly as prescribed.

## R7-4 — the SCAFFOLD arm silently lost the B13 fix

**Conceded in full — the most substantive finding of the round, and it gets the fullest treatment.** The B13 fix (the focal-loss imbalance factor's global prevalence $\bar{p}$ computed from the training shards rather than hardcoded) reached the FedAvg and FedProx local paths but not the SCAFFOLD local path, so the published SCAFFOLD operating points were trained with $\bar{p} = 0.4887$ — the cleaned-substrate prevalence — while the sibling arms used the computed rate. On the roughly two-percent-positive raw substrate the imbalance factor saturates at its clamp under either value, which is why the panel's own reading is "bounded but non-zero", and we adopt that reading. Both halves of the panel's disposition are executed: the one-line call-site fix (`local_train_scaffold` now takes and passes `global_pos_rate`, the round-loop call site supplies it, and a code comment records the provenance of the published numbers), and the one-sentence disclosure in the methods section, which states the asymmetry, its bound, and the alignment — so all three federated arms share one loss definition in the released code, and the paper says plainly that the published SCAFFOLD numbers predate the alignment. Re-running the arm to regenerate its operating points under the aligned loss would require the owner-GPU queue (this revision's verification environment has no torch); that re-run is recorded as owner-side follow-up, and the paper's numbers are unchanged in this revision.
